Ten Years, Three Leaders: Who Runs AI?

소리 내 읽으면 3분쯤 걸린다

낭독하는 법

  1. 문단마다 먼저 들어 보고, 원어민이 읽는 것처럼 소리 내 따라 읽는다.
  2. 내용이 다 이해 안 돼도 괜찮다. 모국어도 그렇게 익혔다. 그냥 끝까지 읽는다.

  5. 한국어로 번역하지 않는다.
내 낭독 저장

Google buys the future

In 2014, Google bought a small AI company in London called DeepMind. It paid more than 500 million dollars. Back then, many people thought Google would own the future of AI.

DeepMind was started by Demis Hassabis. As a young man, he decided that the brain mattered more than physics, because it is the mind that makes sense of the world. He also loved an idea from the physicist Richard Feynman: if you can't build something, you don't really understand it. So he set out to build a machine that thinks like a person. When he started DeepMind in 2010, most investors just laughed.

Musk makes a move

When Google bought DeepMind, Elon Musk got worried. He did not want one company, or one person, to control the future of AI. In late 2015, he helped start a new lab to compete with Google. It was called OpenAI.

For a few years, it really looked like Google's time. In 2016, DeepMind's AlphaGo beat Lee Sedol, one of the best Go players in the world. In 2020, AlphaFold cracked a fifty-year-old puzzle about the shape of proteins. In 2024, Hassabis won a Nobel Prize.

The game changes

Then the game changed. In late 2022, OpenAI released ChatGPT, and Google went into emergency mode. The lab that was built to keep Google in check had jumped ahead.

But the story didn't stop there. Back in 2021, a group of people who had helped build OpenAI's GPT-3 left to start their own company. They called it Anthropic. By May 2026, Anthropic was worth about 965 billion dollars, more than OpenAI.

What it means

So in about ten years, the lead in AI has changed hands twice. First Google, then OpenAI, then Anthropic. If the leader can change that fast, maybe we shouldn't try to picture the world five years from now. It could look very different in just two.

That's what makes this time so exciting to live in.
